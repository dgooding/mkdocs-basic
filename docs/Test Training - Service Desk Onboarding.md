# Test Training - Service Desk Onboarding

This training module gives a new service desk analyst a practical path through the first weeks of support work. It is designed for supervised practice and should be paired with the organization's access, privacy, security, and escalation policies.

## Learning objectives

By the end of the module, the learner should be able to:

- Distinguish incidents, service requests, access requests, and change-related work.
- Create a complete ticket with impact, urgency, affected service, and useful evidence.
- Communicate clearly without exposing confidential information.
- Follow knowledge articles and record when a procedure does not resolve the issue.
- Escalate to the correct resolver group with a concise technical handoff.

## Suggested learning path

### Day 1: Orientation and access

- Review the service desk charter, support hours, priority model, and acceptable-use rules.
- Confirm access to the ticketing system, knowledge base, monitoring view, collaboration channels, and test environment.
- Learn where to find the on-call rota, service ownership list, outage communication process, and security-incident route.

### Week 1: Ticket fundamentals

Practice with non-production examples. For each ticket, capture who is affected, what changed, when it started, business impact, troubleshooting already attempted, and the requested outcome. Use neutral language and avoid blame.

### Week 2: Diagnosis and handoff

Use the knowledge base to test likely causes in a controlled order. State what was checked and what evidence supports the next step. A good handoff lets the resolver group continue without repeating the first hour of investigation.

### Week 3: Independent routine work

Handle low-risk requests under review. Ask for a quality check on prioritization, notes, customer updates, and closure criteria. Do not accept additional privileges simply to make a routine task easier.

## Ticket-writing exercise

Rewrite this weak note:

> User cannot access the system. Please fix.

As a minimum, include:

- Affected user or team, without unnecessary personal data.
- Service and environment.
- Start time and scope of impact.
- Exact error message, if safe to record.
- Checks already completed and their results.
- Current priority, owner, next action, and update time.

## Communication standard

Tell the requester what you know, what you are checking, and when they will hear from you again. Never promise a resolution time that the resolver team has not confirmed. Never request passwords, one-time codes, or secrets in a ticket or chat.

## Escalation practice

Escalate when the issue exceeds the analyst's access or skill boundary, affects multiple users, involves a critical service, suggests a security incident, or has exceeded its response target. Include the evidence, business impact, troubleshooting performed, and a specific question for the receiving team.

## Knowledge check

1. What is the difference between an incident and a service request?
2. Which details help a resolver reproduce a problem?
3. When must a routine password request become a security escalation?
4. What makes a customer update useful?
5. Where should an analyst record a procedure that failed?

Review the answers with a coach using local policy and real examples. Completion means the learner can demonstrate the workflow, not simply finish the course review.
